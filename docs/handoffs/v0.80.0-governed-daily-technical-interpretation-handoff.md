## Released product flow

The governed production chain is:

```text
released Polygon completed-daily Evidence/governance lifecycle
→ qualification
→ governed bridge
→ governed technical execution
→ publication-time technical issuance authority
→ governed Daily Technical Interpretation
→ governed Interpretation result/history
```

Interpretation authenticates retained historical source facts. It does not
reacquire provider data, issue governance records, rerun qualification, convert
the bridge, or rerun the technical analyzer. Original qualification, bridge,
and technical execution contexts remain authoritative; later governance does
not rewrite a prior occurrence. No legacy integrity carrier or fingerprint is
fabricated to adapt this path into legacy Interpretation or Assessment.

## Content, execution identity, and history

`GovernedDailyTechnicalInterpretation` uses schema
`governed_daily_technical_interpretation/v1`. Content binds the five-field source
occurrence, canonical instrument and source trading identity, historical
`analysis_as_of`, distinct snapshot/governed-dataset/research-dataset-content
fingerprints, complete fixed policy, source quality/warnings, states, and
ordered comparisons.

Content identity excludes Interpretation execution metadata. Repeated
deterministic Interpretation of the same authentic technical occurrence has
the same content fingerprint. Different technical occurrences remain distinct
even with equal snapshots because source occurrence identity stays in content.

Repeated executions receive distinct occurrence IDs and envelope fingerprints.
Public construction, copying, or coherent refingerprinting cannot issue an
occurrence or establish trusted history membership.

Chronology is `T <= S <= C <= R`: source technical availability, Interpretation
start, completion, and publication availability, respectively. T must be visible
at S. Timestamps use canonical UTC and R is nondecreasing across publications.
Historical analysis time remains distinct from actual execution/publication.
As-of history visibility is inclusive: `R <= knowledge_as_of`.

History reads validate complete retained history and original upstream
provenance. Missing or corrupt technical, bridge, qualification, governance,
mapping, or material support fails closed. In-memory retention does not imply
durable continuity or replay after history loss/restart.

## Corrective checkpoints

### B1 — Technical publication authority

Status: **CLOSED**.

Checkpoint: `2e07e5588169195b9bf32e9a789f05ea19af8c7c`.

Technical issuance authority exists only at successful Slice 8 publication.
The publisher-owned committed root independently binds the original history
owner/store, namespace, committed state identity and next sequence, original
ordered retained occurrence inventory, and complete immutable issuance bytes.
Retained occurrence identity is pinned as well as content: an equal-content
replacement cannot become the originally issued occurrence.

Each issuance fact is the canonical built-in `bytes` serialization of the final
complete technical `to_dict()` projection, including its envelope fingerprint.
Publication atomically commits the occurrence, issuance fact, and sequence
under the technical lock position after fallible preparation and checks.
Failure creates no issuance fact, consumes no sequence, and clears pending state.

Downstream authentication resolves authentic original issuance against this
root and complete historical provenance rather than trusting value coherence.
There is no first-observation mint/adoption, retrospective backfill/import, or
silent reissue of old history lacking publication-time authority. Empty or
staged state authorizes no occurrence; replaceable compatibility views cannot
replace the committed root.

### B2 — Governed public value isolation

Status: **CLOSED**.

Checkpoint: `cb657bb90d72258dd4f0f95cbf91775f095acffc`.

Governed public graphs contain zero reachable Python Enum instances, including
nested fields, properties, backing values, and refusal reasons. Governed
artifact, policy, and comparison scalar contracts are represented by
`GovernedTechnicalArtifactReference`, `GovernedTechnicalPolicyIdentity`,
`GovernedTechnicalComparisonOperand`, and `GovernedTechnicalComparisonEvidence`.
Categorical values are exact built-in strings; configuration and numeric/Boolean
facts retain their validated scalar contracts without legacy Enum aliases.

Interpretation authoritative history retains an ordered immutable inventory
of complete canonical built-in bytes, not public graphs or mutable decoded
projections. Execute returns and every history read reconstruct fresh validated
public graphs. The execute return is reconstructed and checked from staged final
bytes before those same bytes commit.

Public mutation or coherent refingerprinting cannot rewrite authoritative
bytes, other public reads, internal policy definitions, or legacy Enum
definitions. Complete semantic/source correspondence remains mandatory across
fallible reconstruction and publication seams. Serialized projections and
fingerprint compatibility are preserved.

### B-01 — Pinned owner pending cleanup

Status: **CLOSED**.

Checkpoint: `2b8b31a28dd8545608619ab0366eea55661c986f`.

The release-readiness defect was that replaceable compatibility history could
redirect pending cleanup. Publication now pins `_history_owner`; staging,
pending checks, state write, and cleanup use that same pinned owner. Failed
publication leaves no pending residue and consumes no sequence.

### B-02 — Pinned authority lock

Status: **CLOSED**.

Checkpoint: `ccb0a6e7aa48ae64f454aa56c43f2bdf59157bf2`.

The High-reasoning release review found that `_publish()` used the pinned
authority owner while `_lock_inputs()` had selected the replaceable
compatibility history lock. A swap-back race could therefore publish while
the authoritative owner lock had never been acquired.

The final correction independently pins `_history_owner`. `_lock_inputs()`
acquires `_history_owner._lock`; `_history` cannot select authority locking.
`_check_history()` validates and refuses replacement under pinned-owner locking.
Publication reads, stages, commits, and cleans the pinned owner, and history
reads use pinned-owner state. Persistent replacement and swap-back scenarios
fail closed. The final lock order terminates at that pinned authority owner.

## Final lock order

```text
construction

```

Execution and history reads compose the upstream chain once, then acquire the

recursively call public history methods that reacquire non-reentrant locks.
